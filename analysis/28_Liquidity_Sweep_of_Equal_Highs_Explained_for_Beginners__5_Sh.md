**Core Framework:** Liquidity Sweep + HTF Resistance Confluence + Momentum Shift (RSI < 50) + Change of Character (CHoCH) + Shooting Star Retest

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview

This strategy provides a strict, multi-step checklist ("5 Short Clues") designed to filter out low-probability short trades and capitalize on institutional liquidity sweeps at equal highs (EQH).

The underlying institutional premise is liquidity engineering:
- Retail traders see equal highs as textbook resistance levels and place short orders with stop-losses resting closely above those highs. At the same time, breakout buyers place buy-stop orders above the same highs.
- Smart money drives price momentarily above these equal highs to trigger buy-stops (accumulating sell-side liquidity for large institutional short positions).
- Once filled, price rapidly reverses and closes back below the level, leaving breakout traders trapped and retail shorts stopped out.
- Rather than blindly shorting the sweep, this system waits for **five distinct structural and momentum clues** before triggering an entry.

The core rule of the framework: **"One clue alone is not a signal. Five together? That's your edge."**

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics

### Clue 1: Equal Highs Liquidity Sweep
- Price establishes two or more relatively equal highs (EQH), building up liquidity.
- A subsequent candle pokes above these equal highs, absorbs stop-loss orders, and closes back below the highs.
- This creates an institutional "liquidity grab" (false breakout).

### Clue 2: Key Resistance Alignment
- The sweep occurs at or directly into a major higher-timeframe resistance zone (a prior price pivot where market structure previously turned down strongly).
- This aligns the local liquidity grab with macro institutional order flow.

### Clue 3: RSI Loses and Rejects the 50 Midline
- On the immediate reaction or attempted bounce following the sweep, the Relative Strength Index (RSI) drops below the 50 level.
- Subsequent attempts to rally fail to reclaim the 50 midline, confirming that upside bullish momentum has fully evaporated and bears hold control.

### Clue 4: Change of Character (CHoCH)
- Price breaks below the most recent higher low (the origin of the push that made the sweep high).
- **Critical Rule:** The break must be confirmed by a full candle **body close** below that swing low. A wick through the low does *not* constitute a valid CHoCH.

### Clue 5: Shooting Star on the Retest
- After the CHoCH is confirmed, price retraces upward to retest the broken swing low structure from underneath (old support flipping to new resistance).
- During this retest, price prints a **Shooting Star** candlestick:
  - Small real body at the lower third of the candle range.
  - Long upper wick (at least 2x the body length), showing aggressive seller rejection.
  - Little to no lower shadow.

## 3. Trade Execution Plan

### Directional Bias
- **Bearish / Short**.

### Entry Trigger
- Enter short immediately upon the close of the Shooting Star candle at the retest level, or on the open of the following candle.

### Stop Loss Placement
- Placed just above the swing high of the retest / shooting star wick.
- *Strict Rule:* Must be anchored above the immediate structural rejection wick to account for minor re-tests while keeping risk tightly defined.

### Trade Invalidation
- If any candle prints a full **body close** above the retest high / stop level, the setup is immediately invalidated.
- If price pushes aggressively through the broken low without printing a rejection candle (shooting star), do not enter.

### Take Profit (Target)
- Target the previous major swing low (the origin of the broader bullish impulse preceding the setup).
- Typical reward-to-risk ratio on this structured setup is approximately **2.1R to 2.5R**.

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules

1. **The Trap of Trading Clue 1 in Isolation:**
   - Many retail traders short immediately when equal highs are swept. Without confirmation, price can easily continue trending higher in a strong trending market. Requiring all 5 clues prevents catching a "falling knife in reverse."

2. **Wick vs. Close on CHoCH (Clue 4):**
   - A wick below the higher low is merely another liquidity sweep of the low (which could reverse back upward). Only a decisive **body close** confirms the transition from a bullish market structure to a bearish trend.

3. **RSI 50 Midline as a Trend Regime Filter:**
   - The RSI 50 line acts as the dividing line between bullish and bearish momentum regimes. Even if price wicks lower, if RSI remains buoyant above 50, buyers still have underlying strength. The failure of RSI to cross back above 50 during the bounce is essential proof of seller absorption.

4. **Retest Timing & Location:**
   - The retest should specifically tap into the broken structural level (support-turned-resistance). Entering in "mid-air" before price retests that broken base increases risk and degrades the risk/reward profile.
