# These Two Charts Look the Same. One Is a Breaker Block.

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview
- **Core Concept:** Smart Money Concepts (SMC) structural blocks — differentiating between a **Breaker Block** and a **Mitigation Block**.
- **Market Context & High-Level Premise:**
  - On surface inspection, two reversal chart patterns look identical: price drops from a high, creates an intermediate low, pulls back up to create a swing high (bearish order block), and then explosively breaks upward through that swing high.
  - The decisive institutional discriminator between a **Breaker Block** and a **Mitigation Block** is the presence of an **external liquidity sweep**:
    - **Breaker Block (Chart A):** Formed when price purges a prior swing low (taking sell-side liquidity/stop losses) *before* violently displacing upward through the intermediate high. Because liquidity has been harvested, the failed bearish order block flips into an authoritative institutional support level.
    - **Mitigation Block (Chart B):** Formed when price creates a *failure swing* (higher low) without sweeping the prior swing low before breaking structure upward. The failed zone acts as a mitigation block rather than a breaker block.
  - Institutional significance: In a Breaker Block setup, sell-side liquidity is already cleared; in a Mitigation Block setup, uncollected liquidity remains pooled below the failure swing, creating an active algorithmic magnet that makes the mitigation block vulnerable to future stop runs.

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics

### Chart A: Breaker Block Formation (The High-Probability Setup)
1. **Initial Swing Structure:** Price establishes a swing High, descends to a swing Low, and bounces to print a Lower High (creating a bearish Order Block).
2. **The Liquidity Sweep:** Price turns downward from the Lower High and pierces below the prior swing Low, aggressively sweeping resting sell-side liquidity (retail sell stops / stop losses under the double/swing bottom).
3. **Displacement & Structure Shift (MSS):** Immediately following the sweep, a rapid, high-momentum impulse (Displacement) drives price upward, closing decisively above the intermediate Lower High.
4. **Order Block Failure & Role Flip:** The bearish order block responsible for the initial decline fails and flips into a **Bullish Breaker Block**.
5. **Retest & Confirmation:** Price retraces back into the Breaker Block. The initial touch is rejected, and a bullish confirmation candle forms directly at the zone.

### Chart B: Mitigation Block Formation (The Failure Swing)
1. **Initial Swing Structure:** Price makes a swing High, drops to a swing Low, and pulls back to a Lower High.
2. **The Failure Swing (No Sweep):** Price rolls over but fails to reach or pierce the previous Low, printing a Higher Low (HL). Sell-side liquidity beneath the first low remains completely intact and unmitigated.
3. **Displacement Above Structure:** Price rallies from the higher low and closes above the Lower High.
4. **Mitigation Block Status:** The flipped zone is strictly a Mitigation Block. While it can provide temporary support, institutional algorithms recognize uncollected liquidity pooled below, lowering its structural reliability compared to a true Breaker.

## 3. Trade Execution Plan

### Breaker Block Execution Plan
- **Bias / Market Regime:** Bullish reversal or continuation following a confirmed sell-side liquidity purge.
- **Entry Trigger:**
  - **No Blind Limit Orders:** Do not buy the first touch or wick into the Breaker Block.
  - **Confirmation Candle:** Wait for price to tap the Breaker Block zone, reject lower prices, and print a closed **bullish confirmation candle** (e.g., strong green close or bullish engulfing candle off the zone).
- **Stop Loss:**
  - Conservative: Placed safely below the Breaker Block zone.
  - Aggressive: Placed beneath the low of the retest confirmation candle.
- **Invalidation:** A candle body closing cleanly below the Breaker Block zone cancels the bullish premise immediately.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **TP1:** The high of the displacement rally (external buy-side liquidity).
  - **TP2:** Higher-timeframe swing high / unmitigated Fair Value Gap in premium territory.

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules
- **The Liquidity Sweep Rule:** Without a sweep of prior structure, a block is never a Breaker Block. A Breaker *requires* trapped traders who sold the breakdown or had long stops triggered before price reversed.
- **The Uncollected Liquidity Hazard (Mitigation Blocks):** Because a mitigation block leaves resting stop orders below the failure swing low, market makers frequently use shallow mitigation bounces to lure early buyers before executing a deep liquidity hunt into that uncleared low.
- **First Touch Trap:** Retail traders often set aggressive limit orders on the front edge of broken blocks. Institutional algorithms frequently retrace deep into the block or print a momentary wick through it to balance internal orders before reversing. Always require the retest rejection + confirmation candle close.
- **No Guarantees / Testing Discipline:** Even a textbook sweep does not guarantee a 100% win rate. Backtest both breaker setups and mitigation setups across historical price data to identify specific session and asset consistency.
