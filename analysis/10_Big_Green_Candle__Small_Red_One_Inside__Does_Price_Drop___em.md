# Big Green Candle, Small Red One Inside: Does Price Drop?

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview
- **Core Concept:** Candlestick Anatomy: Harami Patterns, Inside Bars, and Momentum Compression Mechanics.
- **Market Context & High-Level Premise:**
  - When a large trend-expansion candle is immediately followed by a small opposing candle nestled inside its body, traders encounter a **Harami** (Japanese for "pregnant").
  - The appearance of a small candle inside a large candle represents a sudden volatility collapse and momentum stalling. However, retail traders frequently make the fatal mistake of treating a Harami as an immediate reversal trigger.
  - A Harami is strictly a **warning**, not a directional signal. Institutional execution requires directional breakout confirmation and careful structural validation.

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics

### Step 1: Pattern Identification (Bearish & Bullish Harami)
- **Bearish Harami:**
  1. Occurs after an established uptrend.
  2. **Mother Candle:** A large, tall green (bullish) candle displaying aggressive buying.
  3. **Inside Candle:** A small red (bearish) candle whose real body sits entirely within the vertical span of the mother candle's real body.
  4. *Meaning:* Buying momentum has abruptly stalled; demand at higher prices has dried up.
- **Bullish Harami:**
  1. Occurs after an established downtrend.
  2. **Mother Candle:** A large red (bearish) candle reflecting dominant selling.
  3. **Inside Candle:** A small green (bullish) candle whose real body is completely contained inside the mother candle's real body.
  4. *Meaning:* Selling momentum has halted; sellers are failing to push price lower.

### Step 2: The Critical Confirmation Filter
- **Why Standalone Haramis Fail:** A Harami by itself simply reflects indecision. It often resolves as a continuation flag rather than a reversal.
- **Bearish Confirmation:** Wait for a subsequent candle to print a decisive candle **close below the low of the small candle**.
- **Bullish Confirmation:** Wait for a subsequent candle to deliver a decisive candle **close above the high of the small candle**.

### Step 3: Structural Differentiation: Harami vs. Inside Bar vs. Engulfing
- **Harami vs. Inside Bar:**
  - *Harami:* Only the **real body** of the second candle must fit within the real body of the first candle. The wicks of the second candle are permitted to poke out past the high or low of the mother candle.
  - *Inside Bar:* The **entire range** (including upper and lower wicks) of the second candle must remain strictly inside the high and low extremes of the mother candle.
- **Harami vs. Engulfing:**
  - *Harami:* Small candle follows a large candle (Mother bar $\rightarrow$ Baby bar). Signals deceleration.
  - *Engulfing:* Large candle follows a small candle (Baby bar $\rightarrow$ Mother bar). Signals immediate expansion and displacement.

## 3. Trade Execution Plan
- **Bias / Market Regime:** Mean-reversion or trend reversal, but only upon structural breakout confirmation.
- **Entry Trigger:**
  - *Bearish Setup:* Enter short upon a candle close below the low of the small (inside) candle.
  - *Bullish Setup:* Enter long upon a candle close above the high of the small (inside) candle.
- **Stop Loss:**
  - *Bearish Setup:* Placed above the high of the mother candle (or above the local swing high).
  - *Bullish Setup:* Placed below the low of the mother candle (or below the local swing low).
- **Invalidation:**
  - *Bearish Invalidation:* If price rallies and closes back above the high of the big green mother candle, the bearish warning has failed completely.
  - *Bullish Invalidation:* If price breaks down and closes below the low of the big red mother candle, the bullish reversal is invalidated.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **Primary Target:** The base of the mother candle or the nearest key internal liquidity level.
  - **Secondary Target:** Structural swing high/low opposing the entry.

---

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules
- **The "Early Trigger" Trap:**
  - Retail traders see a small red candle inside a big green candle and immediately hit market sell. In strong bullish trends, institutional algorithms often use small inside candles as shallow consolidations before resuming the upward impulse.
- **Body vs. Wick Criteria:**
  - Classical Japanese candlestick rules evaluate Haramis based on the relationship between real bodies. If the small candle's wicks breach the mother bar's highs/lows, it is still a valid Harami, but it is *not* a strict classical Inside Bar.
- **Warning vs. Promise Principle:**
  - "A small candle inside a big one is a warning, not a promise." Institutional traders treat the Harami as an alert to monitor order flow, requiring subsequent candle confirmation to commit capital.
