# 5 Reversal Candlestick Patterns Explained for Beginners

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview
- **Core Concept:** Classical Price Action Reversal Formations & Momentum Depletion Mechanics.
- **Market Context & High-Level Premise:**
  - Candlestick reversal patterns signal a shift in order flow when the dominant party (buyers or sellers) loses momentum and opposing pressure enters the market.
  - A reversal pattern is only valid if preceded by an established trend or wave (a downtrend for bullish reversals, an uptrend for bearish reversals).
  - Patterns like Tweezers test liquidity and find responsive buying/selling at the exact same price level, Haramis represent inside-bar compression indicating trend exhaustion, and three-candle patterns like the Morning Star represent a complete psychological cycle (expansion $\rightarrow$ stall $\rightarrow$ counter-expansion).

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics

### Pattern 1: Tweezer Bottom (Bullish Reversal)
1. **Trend Context:** Established downward price delivery.
2. **Candle 1:** Bearish red candle reaching a swing low.
3. **Candle 2:** Bullish green candle that matches the exact low (or within ticks) of Candle 1.
4. **Structural Meaning:** Equal lows tested twice and firmly defended. Sellers attempted to push lower but encountered aggressive institutional bids at identical price levels.

### Pattern 2: Tweezer Top (Bearish Reversal)
1. **Trend Context:** Established upward price delivery.
2. **Candle 1:** Bullish green candle reaching a swing high.
3. **Candle 2:** Bearish red candle that matches the exact high of Candle 1.
4. **Structural Meaning:** Equal highs rejected twice. Exhaustion of buyers; sell limit orders absorb buy stops, capping price at the exact same ceiling.

### Pattern 3: Bullish Harami (Bullish Inside Bar Exhaustion)
1. **Trend Context:** Preexisting downtrend.
2. **Candle 1 (Mother Bar):** A large, impulsive red candle reflecting strong selling pressure.
3. **Candle 2 (Inside Bar):** A small green candle whose body is completely encompassed within the real body of Candle 1.
4. **Structural Meaning:** Immediate cessation of selling aggression. The failure of bears to push outside Candle 1’s range signals momentum deceleration.

### Pattern 4: Bearish Harami (Bearish Inside Bar Exhaustion)
1. **Trend Context:** Preexisting uptrend.
2. **Candle 1 (Mother Bar):** A large, expansive green candle.
3. **Candle 2 (Inside Bar):** A small red candle whose body sits entirely inside the real body of Candle 1.
4. **Structural Meaning:** Buyer exhaustion and compression. The inability of bulls to continue expanding price reveals declining buyer sponsorship.

### Pattern 5: Morning Star (The 3-Candle Institutional Pivot)
1. **Trend Context:** Established downtrend.
2. **Candle 1 (Selling Wave):** Big red candle demonstrating overt seller dominance.
3. **Candle 2 (The Stall / Indecision):** Small-bodied candle or doji, often gapping down or stalling at lows, showing that selling volume has been absorbed.
4. **Candle 3 (Responsive Buying):** Strong green candle that impulsively rallies and closes decisively above the midpoint (50%) of Candle 1’s real body.
- *Structural Meaning:* The strongest reversal formation, encapsulating the entire turning sequence: Momentum $\rightarrow$ Absorption $\rightarrow$ Counter-Displacement.

## 3. Trade Execution Plan
- **Bias / Market Regime:** Mean-reversion or trend-reversal at key structural higher-timeframe boundaries (Support/Demand for bullish patterns, Resistance/Supply for bearish patterns).
- **Entry Trigger:**
  - *Aggressive Entry:* On the close of the pattern candle (Candle 2 for Tweezers/Harami, Candle 3 for Morning Star).
  - *Conservative Institutional Entry:* Waiting for the **confirmation candle**—a subsequent candle closing decisively in the new direction, confirming order flow transition.
- **Stop Loss:**
  - *Bullish Setups:* Placed strictly below the structural lowest low of the pattern (the equal lows for Tweezer Bottom, or the doji low for Morning Star).
  - *Bearish Setups:* Placed strictly above the highest high of the pattern (the equal highs for Tweezer Top).
- **Invalidation:** A candle body closing beyond the extreme wick of the pattern immediately cancels the reversal bias.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **Primary Target:** The origin of the preceding impulse wave or the nearest internal liquidity pool.
  - **Secondary Target:** Opposing key swing high or low where retail liquidity resides.

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules
- **The "Equal Highs/Lows as Liquidity" Trap:**
  - While classical technical analysis views Tweezer Tops/Bottoms as reversal levels, SMC traders recognize that clean equal highs or equal lows represent pools of liquidity (Double Tops/Bottoms). If price approaches these levels in a strong trending market without prior higher-timeframe liquidity purge, price will often sweep through them rather than reverse.
- **The Confirmation Rule:**
  - A reversal candlestick pattern is merely a clue, not a guarantee. The subsequent candle must demonstrate follow-through in the intended direction to confirm true institutional turnover.
- **Harami vs. Morning Star Reliability:**
  - A Harami alone simply denotes compression/slowing down and can easily evolve into a continuation flag. The Morning Star is inherently far more reliable because Candle 3 actively reclaims 50%+ of the preceding bearish range, proving buying displacement.
