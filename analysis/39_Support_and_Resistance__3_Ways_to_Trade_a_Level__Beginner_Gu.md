# Support and Resistance: 3 Ways to Trade a Level (Beginner Guide)

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview
- **Core Concept:** Institutional Support & Resistance Framework, Liquidity Distribution, Zone Dynamics vs. Single-Line Fallacy, and Retail Breakout Traps.
- **Market Context & High-Level Premise:**
  - Conventional retail technical analysis treats support and resistance as razor-thin, single horizontal price lines. This causes traders to place orders directly on the line or set tight stop losses immediately beneath/above it, making them vulnerable to liquidity engineering.
  - Institutional market participants view Support and Resistance as **broad zones of supply and demand / liquidity pools**. Smart money algorithms capitalize on retail limit orders and breakout orders clustered at these obvious levels.
  - This guide establishes three distinct, systematic methods to trade a key level:
    1. **The Breakout & Retest (Continuation Model):** Riding confirmed institutional momentum after a key level yields.
    2. **The Level Reversal (Fade/Bounce Model):** Trading the rejection off an established boundary when higher-timeframe order flow defends the zone.
    3. **The False Breakout Trap (Liquidity Purge / Double Retest Model):** Exploiting retail stop-runs and inducement before joining institutional expansion.

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics

### Method 1: The Clean Breakout & Retest (Continuation)
1. **Key Zone Identification:** Mark major Support or Resistance as a zone (spanning candle wicks and bodies), not a single coordinate.
2. **Decisive Breakout Candle:** Price approaches resistance and prints a strong, full-bodied candle closing convincingly above the zone (indicating displacement and order flow dominance).
3. **Patience for the Retest:** Do not chase the green breakout candle. Wait for an orderly, low-momentum retracement back down to retest the broken resistance level (now acting as potential support).
4. **Rejection Candle Confirmation:** As price touches the retested boundary, observe price action. The setup requires a clear bullish rejection candle (e.g., lower wick rejecting the level, bullish close).
5. **Entry on Confirmation:** Enter long upon the close of the rejection candle or on the break of its high.

### Method 2: The Level Reversal (Bounce / Fade)
1. **Established Level Approach:** Price expands toward a well-defined resistance zone with drying volume or signs of buyer exhaustion.
2. **Rejection Wick Formation:** Price enters the resistance zone and immediately prints a rejection candle with a noticeable upper wick, indicating active institutional selling absorption.
3. **Confirmation Bearish Close:** Wait for the next candle to confirm the rejection by closing lower (bearish candle breaking below the rejection candle's low).
4. **Execution:** Enter short on the close of the confirming bearish candle.

### Method 3: The False Breakout Trap (The Double Retest Rule)
1. **Retail Breakout Bait:** Price surges above resistance, enticing early breakout buyers. Breakout traders place their stops tightly below the broken resistance level.
2. **Retest 1 (The Trap & Purge):** Price pulls back into the level, briefly appears to hold, but then dips sharply below the broken level, sweeping all the breakout traders' protective stop losses (liquidity engineered).
3. **The Institutional Reclaim:** Price quickly rejects the lower liquidity pool and reclaims back above the resistance zone.
4. **Retest 2 (The High-Probability Entry):** Institutional rule of thumb: **Never enter on Retest 1; always wait for Retest 2.** Retest 2 tests the newly defended support boundary after weak hands have been purged.
5. **Rejection Confirmation:** A bullish rejection candle forms at Retest 2, confirming that smart money has accumulated positions and is ready to expand upward.

## 3. Trade Execution Plan

### A. Breakout Execution Plan
- **Bias / Market Regime:** Strong trending market; HTF directional momentum aligning with the breakout direction.
- **Entry Trigger:** Close of a bullish rejection candle during the retest of broken resistance from above.
- **Stop Loss:** Placed safely below the low of the retest rejection candle and beneath the broken resistance zone.
- **Invalidation:** Candle body closing cleanly back inside the range beneath the broken level.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **TP1:** Next major liquidity pool or previous swing high.
  - **TP2:** Measured move of the prior trading range.

### B. Reversal Execution Plan
- **Bias / Market Regime:** Range-bound market or HTF counter-trend pullback terminating at premium resistance.
- **Entry Trigger:** Close of a bearish confirming candle following a prominent rejection wick inside the resistance zone.
- **Stop Loss:** Placed just above the highest wick tip of the rejection candle/zone.
- **Invalidation:** Price printing a candle close above the resistance zone high.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **TP1:** Midpoint of the range (Equilibrium / 50%).
  - **TP2:** Opposite range boundary (Support zone / Sell-side liquidity).

### C. False Breakout Trap (Double Retest) Execution Plan
- **Bias / Market Regime:** Bullish continuation where a liquidity purge was required to fuel expansion.
- **Entry Trigger:** Bullish rejection confirmation candle printed at **Retest 2**, after Retest 1 executed the stop hunt.
- **Stop Loss:** Below the lowest wick of the Retest 1 purge (below the entire support zone).
- **Invalidation:** Sustained candle close below the entire support structure and stop hunt low.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **TP1:** The high of the initial breakout impulse.
  - **TP2:** Major external buy-side liquidity pool (unmitigated swing highs / daily highs).

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules
- **The "Line vs. Zone" Trap:** Drawing levels as hairline coordinates leads to premature entries and stopped-out positions due to natural spread and liquidity wicks. Always encapsulate the wick extremes and candle bodies into a defined zone.
- **The Retest 1 Hazard:** The vast majority of retail breakout traders buy Retest 1. Institutional algorithms intentionally manipulate Retest 1 to sweep stops just under the level. Waiting for **Retest 2** filters out false continuations and aligns with true institutional accumulation.
- **No Limit Orders on Raw Levels:** Never place blind limit orders directly at support or resistance. Always require visual price action confirmation (wick rejection + directional candle close).
- **Volume & Momentum Context:** True breakouts feature strong expansion bodies and institutional displacement; weak, choppy penetrations through a level are prone to immediate failure and mean reversion.
