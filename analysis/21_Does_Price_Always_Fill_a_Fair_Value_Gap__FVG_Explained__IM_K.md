# Does Price Always Fill a Fair Value Gap? FVG Explained

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview
- **Core Concept:** ICT / Smart Money Concepts (SMC) — Quantitative Statistical Reality of Fair Value Gaps (FVG) and the "Mirror Level" Fallacy.
- **Market Context & Premise:** A study analyzing 29,423 Fair Value Gaps across multiple asset classes (Nasdaq / NQ, Gold / XAUUSD, EUR/USD) and multiple timeframes (1M, 5M, 15M, 1H, 4H) throughout 2024 and 2025. It addresses the common retail belief that "price always comes back to fill an FVG" and exposes why trading blindly toward an open FVG is a losing proposition without confluence.

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics & Statistical Findings
1. **Defining the Fair Value Gap (FVG):** An imbalance created by a fast 3-candle displacement where Candle 1's wick and Candle 3's wick do not touch, leaving an open inefficient price void across Candle 2.
2. **The 92% Fill Reality:** The data reveals that 92.0% of all FVGs are eventually filled within 24 hours. On a specific 5M Nasdaq sample (March 6, 2025), price returned and filled the gap 100 minutes later.
3. **The Mirror Level Symmetry Test:** To determine whether the FVG itself exerts "gravitational pull" or if price is simply random walk expansion, the study measured the distance from the close of Candle 3 to the far edge of the FVG, and projected that identical distance in the *opposite direction* (termed the "Mirror Level").
4. **The Critical Revelation (92.5% Symmetry):** In 15 rigorous test sets across 1M, 5M, 15M, 1H, and 4H on Nasdaq, Gold, and EUR/USD, price reached the opposite "Mirror Level" 92.5% of the time—just as frequently, or even slightly more frequently, than it filled the FVG!
5. **The Structural Conclusion:** Because price travels an equal distance in the opposite direction just as often as it returns to fill the gap, an FVG alone does not possess an inherent directional edge or magnetic pull.

## 3. Trade Execution Plan & Decision Rules
- **Bias / Market Context:** Neutral-to-HTF dependent. Never treat an unmitigated FVG in isolation as a standalone price target or reason to counter-trend trade.
- **Entry Trigger:** 
  * Only use an FVG as an entry zone when price *retraces into it in alignment with higher-timeframe trend and structure* (e.g., HTF Order Block, Liquidity Sweep, or structural Change of Character).
  * Do NOT trade blindly *toward* an unfilled FVG assuming it must fill.
- **Stop Loss:**
  * When using an FVG as a mitigation entry: Place stop loss beyond the swing high/low that created the displacement or beyond the invalidation level (Candle 1's extreme).
- **Invalidation:**
  * A full candle body close through the opposite boundary of the FVG invalidates the gap as an institutional support/resistance shelf.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  * External structural liquidity pools (equal highs/lows, session extremes) rather than internal imbalances.

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules
- **The "FVG Magnet" Trap:** Retail traders often see an open FVG above or below current price and place counter-trend trades expecting price to "fill the gap." Because price moves equal distances in the trend direction 92.5% of the time, trading toward a gap often leads to being caught in strong trend continuation.
- **Rule of Thumb:** "An FVG alone is NOT a reason to trade toward it." Test your own rules and use FVGs strictly as institutional discount/premium mitigation zones within a broader confluence framework.
