# Three Identical Dojis. Only One Matters.

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview
- **Core Concept:** Multi-Timeframe Candlestick Fractal Deconstruction, Lower-Timeframe (LTF) Internal Order Flow, Liquidity Sweeps vs. Meaningless Indecision.
- **Market Context & High-Level Premise:**
  - Standard textbook technical analysis describes a Doji candle as a pure symbol of "market indecision" where buyers and sellers are equally balanced.
  - Institutional analysis recognizes that high-timeframe (HTF) candles are merely aggregate containers of lower-timeframe auction mechanics ($1 \text{ H1 candle} = 4 \times \text{M15 candles}$).
  - Three visual H1 Dojis with identical Open, High, Low, and Close parameters can represent completely opposing market realities:
    - **Doji A ("Empty Inside"):** Passive, low-volume drift. Sweeps no liquidity, breaks no structure. Low probability.
    - **Doji C ("Absorption Without Structure"):** Sharp dip and sharp bounce, followed by sideways decay. Fails to print a structural shift.
    - **Doji B ("Internal CHoCH"):** Liquidity sweep of an established swing low followed by displacement and a Change of Character (CHoCH) across the internal M15 structure. This is the **only high-probability institutional reversal signal**.

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics

### Step 1: HTF Doji Identification
1. Locate an H1 Doji candle appearing at a macro point of interest (POI), key support, or after an extended downtrend.
2. Note that the external geometry of the candle (wicks and body) alone provides zero institutional edge.

### Step 2: LTF Deconstruction (H1 to M15 Fractal Inspection)
Drop down to the M15 timeframe to inspect the four internal candles forming the H1 Doji:
- **Scenario A (Discard):** Price drifts down within the prior range and drifts back up. Sweeps no key swing points and breaks no minor highs. **Action:** Discard.
- **Scenario C (Discard):** Price plunges and bounces aggressively, but then moves sideways into the close without breaking structure. Buyers temporarily absorbed selling but established no dominance. **Action:** Discard.
- **Scenario B (Trade Candidate - Institutional Footprint):**
  1. *The Sweep:* Price drops below equal lows or a prior structural swing low, purging resting sell stops (Sell-Side Liquidity).
  2. *The Displacement:* Aggressive green candle displacement immediately follows the sweep.
  3. *The CHoCH:* Price closes above the last internal lower high on M15, printing a confirmed Change of Character (CHoCH).

## 3. Trade Execution Plan

- **Bias / Market Regime:** Bullish reversal following an internal lower-timeframe liquidity sweep and structure shift.
- **Entry Trigger:**
  - Enter long on the close of the M15 candle that confirms the CHoCH (closing above the last lower high), or upon an M15 retest of the resulting Fair Value Gap / Order Block formed during the internal displacement.
- **Stop Loss:** Placed strictly below the absolute low of the H1 Doji (which marks the LTF liquidity sweep extreme).
- **Invalidation:** A candle body closing below the absolute low of the H1 Doji invalidates the bullish thesis immediately, regardless of how attractive the initial setup appeared.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **TP1:** High of the H1 Doji (internal buy-side liquidity).
  - **TP2:** Higher-timeframe swing high / origin of the H1 downtrend.

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules
- **The Single-Timeframe Illusion:** Trading candlestick shapes in isolation is a primary retail trap. Two identical candles on H1 can conceal completely different order flow dynamics on M15.
- **The Dual-Requirement Rule:** An institutional reversal candle *must* satisfy two criteria on the internal timeframe:
  1. **A Liquidity Sweep:** Stops must be taken to fuel institutional order filling.
  2. **A Change of Character (CHoCH):** Price must demonstrate displacement and close above a structural pivot.
- **Strict Invalidation Discipline:** If subsequent price action penetrates and closes below the low of Doji B, the setup is dead. Never hold a long position when the sweep low fails, as algorithms are actively driving for deeper liquidity.
