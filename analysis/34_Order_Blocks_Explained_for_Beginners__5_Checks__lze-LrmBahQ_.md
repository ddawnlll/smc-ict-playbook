# Order Blocks Explained for Beginners: 5 Checks

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview
- **Core Concept:** Smart Money Concepts (SMC) & ICT Bullish Order Block Validation Framework (The 5-Check Institutional Filter).
- **Market Context & High-Level Premise:**
  - In conventional textbook trading, a bullish order block (OB) is broadly defined as the last bearish (red) candle before an aggressive rally. However, retail traders often incur severe losses by treating every visible order block as an automatic bounce zone.
  - Institutional order flow requires specific structural footprints to confirm that an order block represents genuine institutional capital deployment rather than random market noise.
  - A valid, high-probability order block must satisfy a strict 5-check checklist: taking liquidity, creating displacement with imbalance, breaking market structure, remaining unmitigated (fresh), and trading at a discount price.

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics
1. **Identify the Structural Origin (Check 1: Liquidity Sweep):**
   - Price targets and sweeps a key liquidity pool, such as equal lows (EQH/EQL) or prior swing lows.
   - This action engineers sell-side liquidity (SSL) by triggering retail stop losses and enticing breakout sellers directly into institutional buy limits.
2. **Confirm Aggressive Displacement (Check 2: Fair Value Gap / FVG):**
   - Immediately following the sweep, price violently reverses upward with high-velocity displacement candles.
   - The impulse must leave behind an unclosed **Fair Value Gap (FVG)**, proving an aggressive supply-demand imbalance where retail sellers were completely bypassed.
3. **Verify Market Structure Shift (Check 3: MSS):**
   - The displacement leg must produce a decisive candle close above the most recent lower high (swing high before the liquidity sweep).
   - This formalizes a Market Structure Shift (MSS) / Change of Character (CHoCH), transitioning the market regime from bearish to bullish.
4. **Inspect Mitigation Status (Check 4: Fresh Zone):**
   - Check whether price has already returned to the order block.
   - If price has already tagged the zone once (mitigated, as demonstrated in Chart A), the latent resting orders are likely depleted. The setup demands a **fresh, unmitigated** zone (Chart B).
5. **Calculate Equilibrium and Pricing Tier (Check 5: Discount Pricing):**
   - Measure the entire impulse swing from the swept low to the newly formed displacement high.
   - The order block must reside strictly within the **Discount zone** (below the 50% equilibrium / halfway point of the range). Buying above 50% (premium) offers poor risk-to-reward and higher failure rates.
   - *Rule:* If even one of the five checks is missing, skip the zone entirely.

---

## 3. Trade Execution Plan
- **Bias / Market Regime:** Bullish continuation or reversal following structural transition from discount pricing.
- **Entry Trigger:**
  - Place a limit order at the proximal boundary (top) of the unmitigated bullish order block (or confluent FVG).
  - Alternatively, execute on a lower-timeframe confirmation candle close upon tapping into the discount order block.
- **Stop Loss:**
  - Position the stop loss strictly below the swept swing low (the absolute low that purged liquidity).
- **Invalidation:**
  - A candle close below the swept low invalidates the institutional thesis. If price breaches this level, institutional accumulation failed and the position must be terminated immediately with zero bargaining.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **Primary Target (TP1):** The recent swing high that completed the Market Structure Shift (internal buy-side liquidity).
  - **Secondary Target (TP2):** Major external buy-side liquidity (BSL), previous session highs, or higher-timeframe resistance/premium supply zones.

---

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules
- **The "Mitigated Zone" Trap (Chart A vs Chart B):**
  - Retail traders routinely re-enter order blocks that have already bounced once. The first touch absorbs the bulk of institutional unfilled orders; subsequent retests frequently fail and turn into liquidity runs.
- **The "Freshness Is Not Enough" Fallacy:**
  - Selecting an order block merely because it has never been touched is insufficient. Freshness without a liquidity sweep or market structure shift is often just consolidation within a prevailing trend.
- **The Complete Checklist Discipline:**
  - 1. Sweep (liquidity taken)
  - 2. FVG (displacement verified)
  - 3. MSS (lower high broken with candle body close)
  - 4. Fresh (zero prior touches)
  - 5. Discount (below the 50% midpoint)
  - Treating these five checks as non-negotiable filters weeds out low-probability false signals and ensures alignment with institutional order flow.
