# 5 Continuation Candlestick Patterns Explained for Beginners

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview
- **Core Concept:** Classical Candlestick Continuation Formations / Trend Persistence Mechanics.
- **Market Context & High-Level Premise:**
  - Most candlestick literature focuses heavily on reversal formations, causing novice traders to constantly try picking tops and bottoms against established trends.
  - Continuation patterns illustrate temporary consolidation, profit-taking, or liquidity pauses within an active trend, confirming that dominant market participants are defending their direction rather than exiting.
  - **Prerequisite Condition:** Each continuation pattern strictly requires a well-defined preexisting trend (uptrend for bullish patterns, downtrend for bearish patterns). In a flat or choppy market, these patterns lack predictive value.

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics

### Pattern 1: Rising Three Methods (Bullish Continuation)
1. **Trend Context:** Active preexisting uptrend.
2. **Candle 1 (Displacement):** A long, bullish (tall green) expansion candle reinforcing the trend.
3. **Candles 2–4 (The Pause Inside):** Three consecutive small-bodied candles (often bearish or mixed) that pull back slowly while remaining entirely confined within the high-to-low range of Candle 1.
4. **Candle 5 (The Push):** A tall green candle that surges upward and delivers a decisive close above the close and high of Candle 1.
- *Core Rule:* A temporary pause inside the parent range followed by an impulsive breakout push.

### Pattern 2: Mat Hold (Aggressive Bullish Continuation)
1. **Trend Context:** Strong preexisting bullish momentum.
2. **Candle 1:** A tall green expansion candle.
3. **Candle 2:** A small candle that gaps upward above the close of Candle 1.
4. **Candles 3–4 (The High Pause):** A shallow 2–3 candle consolidation that drifts slightly lower but firmly holds well above Candle 1's low (and often above Candle 1's midpoint).
5. **Candle 5:** A large bullish candle surging to close above all four preceding candle highs.
- *Core Rule:* The pause stays elevated near the highs, indicating far more aggressive buyer sponsorship than the Rising Three Methods.

### Pattern 3: Upside Tasuki Gap (Bullish Gap Continuation)
1. **Trend Context:** Strong bullish uptrend.
2. **Candle 1:** A strong bullish green candle.
3. **Candle 2:** A second bullish green candle that opens with a visible gap up above Candle 1.
4. **Candle 3:** A red (bearish) candle that opens inside Candle 2’s real body and retraces downward into the gap zone.
- *Core Rule:* The red candle closes inside the gap without filling it (the gap remains open). The failure of sellers to close the gap confirms buyer dominance.

### Pattern 4: Falling Three Methods (Bearish Continuation)
1. **Trend Context:** Active preexisting downtrend.
2. **Candle 1 (Displacement):** A long, bearish (tall red) expansion candle.
3. **Candles 2–4 (The Counter-Trend Pause):** Three small bullish/mixed candles that drift upward but stay strictly within the high-to-low range of Candle 1.
4. **Candle 5 (The Push Down):** A tall red candle that closes decisively below the close and low of Candle 1.
- *Core Rule:* A minor counter-trend pause followed by institutional continuation downward.

### Pattern 5: Downside Tasuki Gap (Bearish Gap Continuation)
1. **Trend Context:** Strong bearish downtrend.
2. **Candle 1:** A solid red expansion candle.
3. **Candle 2:** A second red candle that gaps downward below Candle 1.
4. **Candle 3:** A green candle that opens inside Candle 2's body and partially pulls back into the gap window.
- *Core Rule:* The gap is NOT completely filled. The green candle closes within the gap territory, proving sellers maintain full market control.

## 3. Trade Execution Plan
- **Bias / Market Regime:** Pro-trend continuation (Bullish for Rising Three, Mat Hold, Upside Tasuki; Bearish for Falling Three, Downside Tasuki). Never trade against the macro trend.
- **Entry Trigger:**
  - *Standard Entry:* Close of Candle 5 (for Rising/Falling Three and Mat Hold) confirming the breakout of the consolidation range.
  - *Tasuki Gap Entry:* Close of Candle 3 when it fails to close the gap, or on the open of Candle 4 resuming the trend.
- **Stop Loss:**
  - *Bullish Setups:* Placed just below the lowest low of the multi-candle consolidation (or below the low of Candle 1).
  - *Bearish Setups:* Placed just above the highest high of the consolidation (or above the high of Candle 1).
- **Invalidation:**
  - *Rising/Falling Three:* If the consolidation candles close beyond the high/low boundary of Candle 1.
  - *Tasuki Gaps:* A full close that completely fills and breaches the gap voids the setup.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **Primary Target:** Next key swing high/low or liquidity pool in the trend direction.
  - **Secondary Target:** Measured move extension equal to the length of the initial impulse candle (Candle 1).

---

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules
- **The "No Preexisting Trend" Trap:**
  - The most frequent retail failure is identifying 3 small candles inside a large candle within a ranging/choppy market. Continuation patterns are strictly meaningless without an established prior trend.
- **Relative Strength Ranking:**
  - *Mat Hold vs. Rising Three:* A Mat Hold pattern is significantly more powerful than a Rising Three Methods because price does not retrace deep into Candle 1's body; buyers support price at the gap high.
- **Confirmation Requirement:**
  - Candlestick continuation patterns serve as market clues, not standalone holy grails. Institutional traders pair these patterns with key supply/demand levels, order flow volume, and fair value gap context.
