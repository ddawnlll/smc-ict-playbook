## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview

In Smart Money Concepts (SMC) and price action analysis, **Displacement** is defined as an aggressive, rapid, and unbalanced price move initiated by institutional market participants (banks, funds, algorithms) entering large orders. Displacement is the primary footprint indicating that large money has committed to a specific direction.

A common retail misconception is equating any large price move or any break of a swing high with institutional displacement. This video compares three distinct upward moves (Move A, Move B, Move C) that all traverse identical price distance to the exact same high, demonstrating why only one constitutes true displacement:

1. **Move A (The Rejection Spike):**
   - **Characteristics:** One giant, rapid candle that pushes high but aggressively wicks back down, closing near its lower-to-middle half.
   - **Verdict:** False displacement. It represents an aggressive liquidity sweep or strong selling rejection / absorption at overhead resistance, not persistent institutional buying.
2. **Move C (The Corrective Grind):**
   - **Characteristics:** Slow, choppy, stair-stepping price action formed by small, overlapping candles with no speed, low momentum, and wicks on both sides.
   - **Verdict:** False displacement. Lacks institutional urgency and does not create market imbalance. It is typically liquidity building or corrective consolidation.
3. **Move B (True Institutional Displacement):**
   - **Characteristics:** Three consecutive clean, energetic bullish candles with large bodies and short wicks, closing firmly near their highs. The move leaves an open **Fair Value Gap (FVG)** and produces a definitive **Break of Structure (BOS)** with a candle body close above prior swing highs.
   - **Verdict:** True displacement. Demonstrates one-sided aggressive order flow leaving an unmitigated imbalance behind.

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics

True displacement must pass three strict structural checks:

```
[Swing High Resistance Level] ---------------------------------------------
                                        ▲
                                   [Candle 3] Close near high, breaks structure
                                        |
                          [FVG Imbalance] (Candle 1 High < Candle 3 Low)
                                        |
                                   [Candle 2] Large full body, high momentum
                                        |
                                   [Candle 1] Origin of aggressive expansion
                                        ▲
```

### The 3 Core Displacement Validation Checks:

1. **Check 1: Candle Body Dominance & Close Quality**
   - Candlesticks must have large, dominant real bodies with minimal upper and lower wicks.
   - Crucially, candles must close near their extreme highs (for bullish displacement) or extreme lows (for bearish displacement).
   - If a candle prints a long wick opposing the direction of travel (like Move A), it signals immediate liquidity absorption or supply defense, invalidating displacement.

2. **Check 2: Imbalance Creation (Fair Value Gap - FVG)**
   - *Core Rule:* **"No Fair Value Gap = No Displacement."**
   - True displacement moves so fast that price is delivered inefficiently. In a 3-candle sequence, Candle 1's high and Candle 3's low must not overlap, leaving a clear price gap across Candle 2.
   - A move consisting of overlapping candles (like Move C) continuously self-mitigates and balances volume, meaning no institutional imbalance exists.

3. **Check 3: Clean Break of Structure (BOS)**
   - Displacement must achieve structural purpose: it must decisively break beyond a significant prior swing high or low.
   - The breakout must close with the candle **body** beyond the swing pivot level. A mere wick penetration is classified as a liquidity sweep rather than displacement.

## 3. Trade Execution Plan

> **Golden Execution Rule:** *"Displacement is an indicator of who is in control, NOT an entry trigger."*

Retail traders routinely get trapped chasing displacement candles at their peaks. Institutional traders use displacement solely as directional context, waiting for the subsequent pullback to enter at high-probability discount/premium zones.

```
Institutional Entry Sequence:
[Displacement Occurs] ──► [FVG & BOS Confirmed] ──► [Wait for Retracement] ──► [Mitigate FVG / Discount Zone] ──► [Confirmation & Entry]
```

### Execution Protocol:

1. **Context Phase (Displacement Identification):**
   - Confirm Move B profile: 3 large-bodied candles closing near highs, leaving an unmitigated FVG, and closing above the prior swing high (BOS).
   - Do NOT hit market buy at the high of Candle 3.

2. **Retracement Phase:**
   - Allow price to reach exhaustion and initiate a corrective pullback toward the newly created Fair Value Gap.
   - Mark the boundary of the displacement FVG (Candle 1 High to Candle 3 Low) and the Consequent Encroachment (50% midpoint).

3. **Entry Execution:**
   - **Limit Order:** Placed at the top boundary or 50% CE of the FVG.
   - **Confirmation Entry:** Wait for price to tap the FVG and observe lower-timeframe shift (e.g., micro CHoCH or bullish rejection candle) before entering.

4. **Risk & Trade Management:**
   - **Stop-Loss (SL):** Placed protected below the low of Candle 2 (the primary displacement impulse) or beneath the swing low originating the displacement.
   - **Take-Profit (TP):** Target opposing liquidity pools (equal highs, unmitigated supply zones, or major higher-timeframe resistance). Minimum risk-to-reward: 1:2 to 1:3+.

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules

- **The Chasing Trap (FOMO into Expansion):**
  - Buying immediately upon seeing a massive green candle exposes the trader to maximum drawdown, as institutional algorithms routinely retrace into the displacement origin/FVG before continuation.
- **The Rejection Disguise (Move A Trap):**
  - A long wick candle shows that while buyers pushed price up, sellers aggressively pushed it back down before the candle closed. Treating this as bullish momentum leads to buying directly into institutional distribution.
- **The Grind Fallacy (Move C Trap):**
  - A slow crawl upward without gaps or energetic candles indicates low liquidity participation. Grinds frequently end in sharp, violent reversals that wipe out retail breakout traders.
- **Displacement Context Dependence:**
  - Displacement must occur at or following a key structural event (e.g., following a liquidity sweep or leaving a major HTF key zone). Displacement in the middle of a consolidation range has lower predictive power.
