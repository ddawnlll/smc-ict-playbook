# What is a Retest in Trading?

## 1. Strategy & Concept Overview
- **Core Concept:** Classical Price Action & Support/Resistance Role Reversal (Dynamic Trendlines & Horizontal Pivot Levels).
- **Market Context & High-Level Premise:**
  - When price breaches an established boundary—whether a dynamic trendline or a static horizontal support/resistance zone—traders face the dilemma of distinguishing between a genuine structural breakout and a trap (fakeout).
  - A **Retest** is the price action mechanism where the market breaks out, expands away, and subsequently rotates back to test that exact same boundary from the opposite side.
  - **The Principle of Role Reversal:**
    - Broken support flips to become new resistance.
    - Broken resistance flips to become new support.
  - The retest serves as an objective validation filter: if price rejects the retested boundary, the breakout is confirmed; if price pushes straight back through the boundary, the breakout was a fakeout.

---

## 2. Step-by-Step Setup Mechanics
1. **Identify an Established Boundary:**
   - **Rising Dynamic Trendline:** Price respects an upward-sloping trendline with multiple touchpoints acting as dynamic support.
   - **Descending Dynamic Trendline:** Price respects a downward-sloping trendline acting as dynamic resistance.
   - **Horizontal Support/Resistance:** Price repeatedly bounces off an established horizontal level (e.g., multi-touch support floor).
2. **Observe the Initial Breakout:**
   - Price breaks decisively through the boundary (e.g., closing below the ascending trendline or slicing through horizontal support).
   - "Before you do anything, you need to check one thing first": Do not enter blindly on the initial break.
3. **Wait for the Retest Leg:**
   - Allow price to travel away from the break point, exhaust its momentum, and pull back toward the broken line.
   - Price touches the underside of the broken support level (or the top of broken resistance).
4. **Evaluate the Two Retest Outcomes:**
   - **Outcome 1 (Valid Role Reversal - The Real Break):** Price encounters aggressive opposing order flow at the line and gets firmly rejected, printing rejection wicks. The line has officially flipped (Support $\rightarrow$ Resistance).
   - **Outcome 2 (Fakeout - The Trap):** Price fails to hold the level and pushes straight back inside the original structure. The breakout was false; the original support still holds.
5. **Execution Confirmation:**
   - Enter strictly once the retest prints definitive rejection away from the level in the direction of the breakout.

---

## 3. Trade Execution Plan
- **Bias / Market Regime:**
  - Shift to a **Bearish Bias** when support (trendline or horizontal) breaks, is retested, and prints confirmed rejection.
  - Shift to a **Bullish Bias** when resistance (trendline or horizontal) breaks, is retested, and prints confirmed rejection.
- **Entry Trigger:**
  - Wait for price to touch the flipped level during the retest phase.
  - **Confirmation Trigger:** Enter on the close of a rejection candle (e.g., pin bar, bearish engulfing candle off flipped resistance, or bullish hammer off flipped support).
- **Stop Loss:**
  - Placed safely on the invalidation side of the retested level (e.g., just above the rejection wick high of the retest on a short setup).
- **Invalidation:**
  - A candle close back inside the original boundary (confirming a fakeout) immediately invalidates the trade setup.
- **Take Profit Target:**
  - **Primary Target (TP1):** The local swing low/high formed during the initial breakout leg prior to the retest.
  - **Secondary Target (TP2):** Major structural support/demand levels (for shorts) or resistance/supply levels (for longs) further down the trend.

---

## 4. Key Nuances, Traps & Rules
- **The Breakout FOMO Trap:**
  - Amateurs jump in the moment a line is breached. This is where market makers create liquidity traps (fakeouts). By waiting for the retest, traders filter out the vast majority of false breakouts.
- **The "Role Reversal" Verification Rule:**
  - A broken level does not automatically guarantee resistance. It only becomes verified resistance when price arrives at the line and displays active rejection.
- **"Most Breakouts Get a Retest, But Not All":**
  - While extreme runaway momentum breakouts occasionally leave without a retest, professional risk management accepts missed outliers to guarantee high-probability, low-risk entries on verified retests.
- **Horizontal vs. Trendline Application:**
  - Retests operate identically across rising trendlines, falling trendlines, and horizontal levels. However, horizontal levels represent static volume agreements, often providing cleaner retest reactions than subjective diagonal trendlines.
